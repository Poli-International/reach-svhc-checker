## What is REACH SVHC Pigment Checker?

The REACH SVHC Pigment Checker is a free web tool that screens tattoo ink ingredients, body jewelry materials, and studio product components against the ECHA (European Chemicals Agency) Candidate List of Substances of Very High Concern. The tool uses a curated database of 80 high-relevance substances specifically selected for their presence in body art products, drawn from the official list of 244 SVHC substances.

The tool provides two screening methods:
- **Single Ingredient Search:** Enter a substance name, CAS number, or EC number to check if it appears on the SVHC list.
- **Bulk SDS Block Scan:** Paste an entire Safety Data Sheet Section 3 or ingredient list to scan multiple ingredients simultaneously. The tool automatically extracts CAS numbers and ingredient names, then cross-references each against the SVHC database.

Results display hazard classifications (CMR, PBT, Endocrine Disruptor), body art relevance ratings (high, medium, low), product categories where the substance is commonly found, and direct links to the official ECHA substance page.

## Who Should Use This Tool

**Tattoo Studio Owners and Managers:** Verify that all inks, aftercare products, and studio supplies comply with EU REACH regulations before use on clients.

**Body Piercers and Jewelry Retailers:** Screen piercing jewelry alloys and materials for nickel, cadmium, lead, and other SVHC metals commonly found in low-grade jewelry.

**Tattoo Ink Manufacturers and Distributors:** Perform quick ingredient checks during product development and quality control to identify potential SVHC issues before market release.

**Regulatory Compliance Officers:** Conduct preliminary screenings of product inventories and supplier documentation to identify high-priority substances requiring further investigation.

**Tattoo Artists:** Check individual ink bottles and pigments before use, especially when working with new or unfamiliar brands.

## How to Use the REACH SVHC Pigment Checker

### Single Ingredient Search

1. **Navigate to the tool** at https://poliinternational.com/tools/reach-svhc-checker/
2. **Ensure the "Search ingredient" tab is active** (default view)
3. **Enter your query** in the input field. You can use:
   - An ingredient name (e.g., "Nickel", "cobalt blue", "DEHP")
   - A CAS number (e.g., "7440-02-0", "117-81-7")
   - An EC number
   - Common aliases (e.g., "BaP" for benzo[a]pyrene)
4. **Click "Check"** or press Enter
5. **Review the result:**
   - If flagged: A red result card appears with hazard badges, relevance rating, and detailed information
   - If not found: A green card confirms the substance is not in the curated SVHC list
6. **Click the card header** to expand/collapse detailed information including CAS numbers, EC number, hazard classification, and product categories

### Bulk SDS Block Scanning

1. **Click the "Paste SDS block" tab**
2. **Paste your ingredient list** or SDS Section 3 into the text area. The tool accepts:
   - INCI lists (e.g., "Aqua, Glycerin, Iron oxide CI 77491")
   - SDS Section 3 composition blocks
   - Raw ingredient lists with CAS numbers
   - Mixed formats with names and numbers
3. **Click "Scan for SVHCs"** to begin analysis
4. **Review the scan results:**
   - **Stats bar:** Shows number of flagged substances, non-flagged substances, and total ingredients parsed
   - **Alert banner:** Warning if SVHCs detected, informational message if none found
   - **Flagged substances section:** Each flagged substance displayed as an expandable card
   - **Not in SVHC list section:** Ingredients that didn't match any SVHC entry
5. **Use "Clear"** to reset the input and results

### Understanding Result Cards

Each flagged substance card contains:
- **Status icon:** Warning symbol (⚠️)
- **Substance name and CAS number(s)**
- **Hazard badge:** Shows the primary concern (CMR, PBT, Carcinogenic, etc.)
- **Relevance badge:** High risk (red), Med risk (yellow), Low risk (neutral)
- **Expanded details:** Hazard classification, EC number, match method, found in body art products, also known as aliases
- **Action links:** Direct link to ECHA substance page and full SVHC list

## Real-World Use Cases

### Use Case 1: Checking a New Tattoo Ink Batch

**Scenario:** A studio owner receives a new shipment of black tattoo ink and wants to verify it doesn't contain restricted PAHs.

**Steps:**
1. Open the bulk scan tab
2. Paste the SDS Section 3: "Carbon Black (Pigment Black 7), Aqua, Glycerin, Isopropyl Alcohol, Benzo[a]pyrene < 0.1%"
3. Click "Scan for SVHCs"
4. **Result:** The tool flags Benzo[a]pyrene (BaP) as a high-relevance SVHC with CMR classification
5. **Action:** Contact the supplier for documentation showing BaP levels below the regulatory threshold, or switch to a compliant ink brand

### Use Case 2: Screening Body Jewelry Materials

**Scenario:** A piercer wants to verify that their titanium jewelry doesn't contain nickel contamination.

**Steps:**
1. Open the single search tab
2. Enter "Nickel" or "7440-02-0"
3. Click "Check"
4. **Result:** Nickel appears as a high-relevance SVHC, flagged as CMR
5. **Action:** Request mill certificates from the jewelry supplier confirming nickel-free titanium alloy (Ti-6Al-4V ELI)

### Use Case 3: Auditing Studio Product Inventory

**Scenario:** A studio manager audits all products for REACH compliance before an EU regulatory inspection.

**Steps:**
1. Collect SDS documents for all inks, aftercare products, and cleaning supplies
2. Open the bulk scan tab
3. Paste combined ingredient lists from multiple products
4. Click "Scan for SVHCs"
5. **Result:** The tool identifies phthalates (DEHP, DBP) in ink bottle packaging and BPA in aftercare product containers
6. **Action:** Replace PVC gloves with nitrile, switch to BPA-free aftercare products, and document all changes for the inspection

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What does SVHC stand for?

SVHC stands for "Substances of Very High Concern." These are chemicals identified by the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) as having serious and often irreversible effects on human health or the environment. Categories include carcinogens, mutagens, reproductive toxicants (CMR), persistent bioaccumulative toxic substances (PBT), and endocrine disruptors.

### How often is the SVHC list updated?

The ECHA Candidate List is updated twice per year, typically in January and July. The tool displays the last update date (currently 2026-01-14) and the total official substance count (244 substances). The curated body-art edition includes 80 high-relevance substances selected for tattoo and piercing applications.

### Does a "not found" result mean the product is safe?

No. A "not found" result means the substance is not in the curated body-art SVHC database. This does not constitute regulatory clearance. Always verify against the official ECHA Candidate List and consult your supplier's Safety Data Sheet for legally binding information. The tool is a screening aid, not a substitute for professional regulatory advice.

### Can I check multiple ingredients at once?

Yes. Use the "Paste SDS block" tab to paste entire ingredient lists or SDS Section 3 blocks. The tool automatically extracts CAS numbers and ingredient names, then checks each against the SVHC database. Results include a stats bar showing flagged vs. non-flagged substances and separate sections for each category.

### What types of CAS numbers does the tool accept?

The tool accepts standard CAS Registry Numbers in the format XXX-XX-X (e.g., 7440-02-0 for nickel). It also accepts EC numbers and common substance names. The bulk scanner uses a regex pattern to extract CAS numbers from text, so ensure CAS numbers are properly formatted for best results.

### How is body art relevance determined?

Body art relevance is assigned based on how commonly the substance appears in tattoo inks, body jewelry, aftercare products, or studio supplies. Ratings include:
- **High:** Frequently found in body art products (e.g., nickel in jewelry, PAHs in black inks)
- **Medium:** Occasionally found or used in manufacturing processes
- **Low:** Rarely found but included for completeness

### What should I do if I find an SVHC in my product?

If the tool flags an SVHC in your product:
1. Verify the finding against the official ECHA Candidate List
2. Contact your supplier for the Safety Data Sheet and documentation
3. Determine if the substance is present above the regulatory threshold (typically 0.1% w/w)
4. If above threshold, consider switching to a compliant alternative
5. Document all findings and actions for regulatory compliance records

### Is this tool a substitute for professional regulatory advice?

No. This tool is a preliminary screening aid only. It does not provide legally binding information. Always consult with a regulatory compliance professional and verify against the official ECHA Candidate List for definitive compliance determinations.

### Can I use this for non-tattoo products?

While the tool is optimized for body art products, it can screen any ingredient list against the SVHC database. However, the body art relevance ratings and "found in" categories are specific to tattoo and piercing applications. For non-body-art products, consult the full ECHA list directly.

### How does the bulk scan extract ingredients?

The bulk scanner uses regex pattern matching to identify CAS numbers (format XXX-XX-X) and ingredient names from the pasted text. It then cross-references each extracted item against the SVHC database. The tool prefers CAS number matches over name matches for accuracy. Deduplication ensures each substance appears only once, with CAS matches taking priority over name matches.
